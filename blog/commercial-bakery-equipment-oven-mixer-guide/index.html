Section 01

Spiral Mixers vs. Planetary Mixers: Protecting Gluten & Motor Gears

One of the most frequent mechanical failures in Sri Lankan bakeries is burning out a planetary mixer's gearbox by kneading heavy, low-hydration bread or pizza dough batches. Understanding the mechanical difference between spiral and planetary mixers in our Bakery Equipment Catalogue is vital:

  • Dual-Speed Spiral Dough Mixers (20L to 130L+): Both the stainless steel bowl and the spiral hook rotate simultaneously around a central breaker bar. This develops a strong, silky gluten network quickly while keeping dough friction heat low—mandatory for bread loaves, buns, baguettes, pizza dough, and yeast-raised donuts.
  • 3-Speed Planetary Mixers (10L to 60L): The bowl remains stationary while a replaceable attachment (wire whip, flat beater, or dough hook) orbits around the bowl. Essential for whippingmeringues, sponge cake batters, buttercreams, ganaches, and cookie doughs.

Section 03

Lamination & Portioning: Dough Sheeters, Dividers & Rounders

To scale production without quadrupling skilled pastry labor, commercial bakeries automate two labor-intensive steps:

  1. Hydraulic & Semi-Automatic Dough Dividers / Rounders (30-Part / 36-Part): Divides a bulk dough mass into 36 identical weight-calibrated dough balls and rounds them into tight gluten spheres in under 15 seconds.
  2. Floor or Countertop Reversible Dough Sheeters: Rolls butter-laminated croissant, puff pastry, and fondant sheets down to uniform millimeter thicknesses without tearing butter layers.
Section 04

Controlled Fermentation: Proofing Cabinets & Retarder Proofers

Leaving dough trays out on open racks in Sri Lanka causes uneven skin crusting and erratic yeast fermentation. Dedicated 16-tray and 32-tray stainless steel proofer cabinets maintain exact temperature (+35°C to +38°C) and relative humidity (75%–85% RH), ensuring uniform oven spring across every batch.

Section 06

Frequently Asked Questions: Commercial Bakery Equipment

Should a pastry kitchen stay air-conditioned when running deck and convection ovens?

Yes. Laminated doughs (croissants and puff pastry) must be sheeted at room temperatures between 18°C and 22°C so the butter layer does not melt into the flour. We isolate the hot oven line under a dedicated Type-II heat extraction hood separated from the chilled marble/SUS304 pastry prep room.

Are gas or 3-phase electric deck ovens better for Sri Lankan bakeries?

Three-phase electric deck ovens provide ultra-precise top-and-bottom infrared element control for fine patisserie, while gas-fired deck ovens (using digital ignition and minimal 220V control power) offer lower running fuel costs where 3-phase transformer capacity is limited.
