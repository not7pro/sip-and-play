Section 01

The 1.0-Meter Utility Rough-In Standard

A commercial kitchen layout is only as functional as the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing (MEP) infrastructure hidden beneath the floor tiles and above the ceiling plenum. As outlined in our parent pillar guide on Commercial Kitchen Design & Layout Planning and formalized under Clause 06 of our Master Terms, every utility termination point must be brought within 1.0 meter of its corresponding appliance connection point before equipment rigging begins.

Section 02

Type-I vs. Type-II Exhaust Hoods, Duct Velocity & Make-Up Air

Commercial kitchen ventilation has played a decisive role in complex builds such as our 33,000 sq ft Chinese Mega Kitchen in Colombo. Engineers classify commercial exhaust canopies into two distinct categories:

Ventilation Parameter Type-I Grease & Smoke Canopy Type-II Heat & Condensate Canopy
Target Appliances Wok ranges, open burners, deep fryers, charbroilers, griddles, solid-fuel/tandoor ovens Hood/conveyor dishwashers, steam kettles, non-grease pastry deck ovens
Filtration & Construction 1.2mm–1.5mm SUS304 welded hood with 45° stainless baffle grease filters & collection cup Condensate gutter baffle without grease filters; directs steam vapor away from ceilings
Canopy Overhang Minimum 150mm overlap beyond cooking appliance edges on all open sides 100mm–150mm overlap above dishwasher hood or oven door opening
Make-Up Air (MUA) Ratio 80%–85% filtered fresh supply air (+15%–20% negative pressure balance) 70%–80% fresh air replacement to prevent humidity condensation
Section 03

Electrical Distribution: Single-Phase vs. 3-Phase Load Balancing

Sri Lankan commercial facilities operate on a 230V single-phase / 400V three-phase 50Hz grid. High-draw thermal machinery must never share undersized domestic circuits:

  • Three-Phase (380–400V, 50Hz, 5-Pin Rotary Isolators): Required for electric deck ovens, combi steamers (12kW–35kW), conveyor/hood dishwashers with booster heaters, walk-in cold room condensing units, and heavy spiral mixers.
  • High-Amperage Single-Phase (220–230V, 32A Commando/Rotary Isolators): Required for commercial multi-boiler espresso machines such as the 4200W INNOSONG ZS3202 and 6200W T&Z KT3-2A or T&Z VK-2 dual-head tea systems.
  • Standard 13A/16A IP65 Splash-Proof Sockets: Used for upright reach-in chillers, under-counter counters, INNOSONG ZS3101/ZS3105 (2400W), LEHEHE DZ23-1Y (2200W), and EVEBOT EB-FC1 (60W) beverage printers.
Section 04

Potable Water Pressure, Filtration, Floor Gullies & Grease Traps

Improper drainage slope and missing grease interceptors are the primary causes of foul odors and blocked municipal sewer connections in restaurants:

  • Minimum 2.5 Bar Potable Water Pressure: Solenoid valves inside combi ovens, ice makers, and rotary-pump espresso bars require a steady 2.5 to 4.0 bar supply pressure.
  • SUS304 Floor Drainage Channels: Installed in front of tilting bratt pans, wok suites, and dishwashing zones with removable perforated stainless steel strainer baskets and anti-slip grating.
  • Grease Interceptors (Grease Traps): All prep sinks, pot-wash sinks, and wok floor troughs must discharge through a properly sized SUS304 under-sink or central underground grease trap to capture solidified fats, oils, and grease (FOG) before entering the main drain stack.
Section 05

LPG Gas Manifold Architecture & Emergency Safety Interlocks

Commercial gas cooking lines require an external, ventilated LPG cylinder manifold room with duty and standby cylinder banks, automatic changeover valves, and a two-stage pressure regulation system. Inside the kitchen, the main gas line must pass through an electromagnetic solenoid shut-off valve interlocked with both the exhaust hood fan (so gas cannot flow unless the exhaust fan is running) and low-level LPG gas leak detectors mounted 150mm above floor level.

Section 06

Frequently Asked Questions: Kitchen MEP Engineering

Why is a kitchen kept under slightly negative air pressure?

By supplying 80%–85% make-up air against 100% exhaust extraction, the kitchen remains at a slight negative pressure relative to the dining room. This ensures cooking aromas, smoke, and steam never drift out into guest seating areas.

What happens if water pressure drops below 2.5 bar during peak service?

Low water pressure starves commercial espresso machine rotary pumps (causing pump cavitation) and triggers water-inlet fault errors on combi ovens and commercial dishwashers. A dedicated booster pump and pressure tank should be installed on the kitchen cold water feed.
