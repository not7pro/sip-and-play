Section 01

The 3-Cabinet Crossover Rule: When to Switch to a Walk-In Cold Room

A standard 4-door upright commercial chiller holds approximately 1,000 to 1,200 liters of net usable shelf volume. Once a restaurant, hotel, or catering facility requires more than three 4-door upright cabinets (3,600+ liters) for bulk storage, a modular cam-lock walk-in cold room delivers double the storage volume on the same floor footprint while rejecting zero compressor heat into the kitchen workspace.

In high-throughput commercial kitchens—even those anchored by walk-in cold rooms like our 33,000 sq ft Chinese Mega Kitchen—both systems work together: walk-in cold rooms store bulk crates at the receiving bay, while upright reach-in and under-counter chillers store portioned daily mise en place on the prep and hot lines. Section 03

Civil Slab Preparation & Combination Chiller-Freezer Rooms

For space-constrained urban Colombo properties, Sip & Play frequently engineers shared-wall combination walk-in rooms where a +2°C chiller ante-room leads directly into a −20°C deep freezer chamber. This reduces total insulated wall panels and prevents warm humid kitchen air from rushing directly into the sub-zero freezer whenever the door opens.

Section 04

Frequently Asked Questions: Cold Rooms vs. Reach-In Fridges

Can a modular walk-in cold room be disassembled and relocated if we move premises?

Yes. Because cam-lock polyurethane panels interlock mechanically without permanent masonry walls, a modular walk-in cold room can be unbolted, transported to a new facility, and re-commissioned.

Why do walk-in freezers require under-floor heating mats or ventilated sub-slabs?

Operating at −20°C continuously can freeze moisture inside the structural concrete slab beneath the insulated floor panels over several years, causing frost heave. Installing a depressed insulated floor with a low-wattage heater mat or air-gap sub-base prevents slab cracking.
